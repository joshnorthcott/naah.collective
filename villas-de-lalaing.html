Villas de Lalaing

Near Acanceh, Yucatán · 35 minutes from Mérida

For sale · 6.7 hectares

Sixteen and a half acres of private Yucatán countryside with everything already on it: a finished residence, an independent studio suite, a guest casita, a plunge pool, a tropical orchard, a waterfall, a medicinal garden and a lookout tower rising above the jungle canopy. Off-grid, fully solar, and running on its own two wells.

This is not raw land and it is not a house with a garden. It is an established private estate of approximately 6.7 hectares — 16.6 acres — held as a main parcel of 15.8 acres plus two further separately registered parcels. A Dutch family spent several years turning it from bush into what it is now, and the work shows in the places that usually take longest: mature planting, finished paths, working infrastructure, water where it is needed.

The main residence runs to about 108 m² of indoor living space — two bedrooms, two bathrooms, and a covered outdoor terrace under a palapa that does most of the actual living. Above it, reached by its own outdoor staircase, sits an independent studio suite with a private bathroom, a kitchenette and a large terrace of its own. A separate guest casita of about 24 m², also with its own bathroom and kitchenette, stands apart in the grounds. Three distinct places to sleep, each genuinely private from the others.

What makes the estate unusual, though, is that it owes nothing to anyone. Sixteen solar panels — eight ground-mounted, eight built into the carport roof — feed 30 kWh of battery storage, with a second solar system serving the casita and solar hot water on top of that. Two private wells with pumps supply the house and the irrigation. Starlink handles the internet. There is no homeowners' association and no monthly utility bill.

The residence & the casita

Two bedrooms and two bathrooms across roughly 108 m² of indoor space, with the covered terrace doing the rest. The casita stands separately, on its own solar supply.

The studio suite & the tower

A private outdoor staircase climbs from the garden to the studio suite on the upper floor — an open-plan living and sleeping room, a private bathroom, a kitchenette, and a large terrace looking out over the grounds. It is the obvious answer for family who stay a season, for a caretaker, or for guests who pay.

From that terrace a spiral staircase reaches a second, larger terrace above, and from there a short climb takes you into the lookout tower itself, standing clear of the treetops. Three levels of outdoor living stacked one above the other, ending above the jungle.

Outside

The plunge pool sits among mature tropical planting rather than on an open slab, with palapas and shaded terraces around it. Walking paths run out from the gardens into the estate: past the cascada, through the medicinal and herbal garden with its fountain, into an established orchard of roughly thirty fruit trees, past the enclosed paddock, and on into genuine jungle. Irrigated lawn where lawn makes sense; native vegetation where it does not.

The gardeners and handymen who built and maintain the grounds would like to stay on.

Independent by design

Power, water and connectivity are the three things that decide whether a rural property in Yucatán is comfortable or merely romantic. All three are already resolved here, and they are the reason the estate carries no monthly utility bill at all.

Acanceh and the countryside

The estate sits about eight minutes from Acanceh, a working Yucatán town with a Mayan pyramid in the middle of its main square, and roughly thirty-five minutes from Mérida by road. The international airport is about fifty minutes away and the Gulf beaches about fifty-five. Several of the restored haciendas are only minutes from the gate.

This is the cenote country south of the city — traditional Mayan villages, fertile ground that takes tropical planting without argument, and the birdlife and butterflies that come with leaving jungle where it stands. Everything needed day to day remains within a comfortable drive, which is the whole point of being thirty-five minutes out rather than two hours.
